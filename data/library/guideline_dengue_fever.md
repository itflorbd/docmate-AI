# Clinical Practice Guideline: Clinical Management of Dengue Infection & Warning Signs
**Source Title**: Bangladesh DGHS & WHO Clinical Guidelines for Dengue Management
**Publication Year**: 2024
**Evidence Level**: Ia
**License**: Open Access / Clinical Demo Material

---

### Section 1: Clinical Presentation & Diagnostic Phases
Dengue fever is an acute arboviral febrile illness transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. The illness typically follows three clinical phases: febrile, critical, and recovery. The febrile phase lasts 2 to 7 days, characterized by sudden high fever, severe retro-orbital headache, diffuse arthralgia and myalgia (breakbone fever), flushing, and maculopapular rash. A positive tourniquet test or early leukopenia (WBC < 4000/μL) often precedes platelet drop. The critical phase typically occurs between days 3 and 7 of fever around the time of defervescence, during which plasma leakage can lead to hypovolemic shock.

### Section 2: Clinical Warning Signs for Severe Dengue
Patients must be evaluated daily for clinical warning signs signaling impending plasma leakage or severe hemorrhage:
1. Severe persistent abdominal pain or tenderness.
2. Persistent vomiting (≥ 3 episodes within 12 hours).
3. Clinical fluid accumulation (pleural effusion or ascites on ultrasound/physical exam).
4. Mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, gingival bleed, hematuria, or menorrhagia).
5. Lethargy, restlessness, or altered mental status.
6. Hepatomegaly (> 2 cm below the right costal margin).
7. Rapid progressive increase in hematocrit concurrent with rapid decrease in platelet count (< 100,000/μL).
The emergence of any warning sign mandates immediate hospitalization and intravenous fluid therapy.

### Section 3: Antipyretic Safety & Absolute Contraindication of NSAIDs
Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) is the sole recommended antipyretic for symptom relief in dengue infection. The adult dose is 500 mg to 1000 mg orally every 6 hours as needed, with a strict maximum limit of 3000 mg (3 grams) in 24 hours to prevent hepatotoxicity. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) such as Ibuprofen, Diclofenac, Naproxen, Ketorolac, and Aspirin are strictly contraindicated in suspected or confirmed dengue due to their antiplatelet effects and high risk of precipitating catastrophic gastrointestinal hemorrhage and acute gastric erosions. Intramuscular injections must never be administered due to severe hematoma risk.

### Section 4: Fluid Management Protocol & Outpatient Monitoring
Outpatient fluid intake should be maintained at oral maintenance rates (approximately 2.5 to 3 liters per 24 hours for adults), utilizing Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS), coconut water, fruit juice, and soup rather than plain water alone to avoid hyponatremia. In hospitalized patients with warning signs without shock, isotonic crystalloids (Normal Saline 0.9% or Ringer's Lactate) should be initiated at 5 to 7 mL/kg/hour for 1 to 2 hours, then titrated downward based on serial hematocrit and vital signs.
