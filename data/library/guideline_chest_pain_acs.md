# Clinical Practice Guideline: Evaluation and Triage of Acute Chest Pain & Acute Coronary Syndrome
**Source Title**: International Emergency Cardiology Consensus & Triage Protocol
**Publication Year**: 2024
**Evidence Level**: Ia
**License**: Open Access / Clinical Demo Material

---

### Section 1: Initial Presentation & Red-Flag Escalation Criteria
Acute chest pain requires immediate, systematic clinical triage to exclude life-threatening cardiovascular and pulmonary emergencies. Any patient presenting with acute substernal chest discomfort described as pressure, crushing, squeezing, or heaviness, especially when radiating to the left arm, shoulder, neck, or jaw, accompanied by diaphoresis, dyspnea, nausea, or syncope, must trigger an urgent red-flag alert. Immediate 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG) must be acquired and interpreted within 10 minutes of initial presentation, alongside high-sensitivity cardiac troponin testing.

### Section 2: Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI vs NSTEMI / Unstable Angina)
STEMI (ST-segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction) is diagnosed when new ST-segment elevation is present in two contiguous leads (≥ 1 mm in limb leads or ≥ 2 mm in precordial leads) or a new left bundle branch block. STEMI requires immediate emergency reperfusion therapy via primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) within 90 to 120 minutes, or emergency thrombolysis if PCI is unavailable within 120 minutes. Patients with dynamic ST depression, T-wave inversion, or elevated troponin without diagnostic ST elevation are classified as NSTEMI, requiring urgent inpatient admission, risk stratification, and early invasive angiography.

### Section 3: Emergency Pharmacological Triage Regimen
Unless active gastrointestinal bleeding or documented severe aspirin anaphylaxis is present, non-enteric coated Aspirin 300 mg should be chewed and swallowed immediately upon recognition of suspected acute coronary syndrome. For persistent ischemic chest discomfort and systolic blood pressure > 90 mmHg, sublingual Glyceryl Trinitrate (Nitroglycerin) 0.5 mg tablet or spray may be administered every 5 minutes up to 3 doses under continuous blood pressure monitoring. Sublingual nitrates are strictly contraindicated if the patient has taken a phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE-5) inhibitor (such as Sildenafil within 24 hours or Tadalafil within 48 hours), or in suspected right ventricular infarction.

### Section 4: Non-Cardiac Differential Diagnoses & Differentiation
When acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, pulmonary embolism, and tension pneumothorax have been excluded:
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is suggested by burning retrosternal discomfort aggravated by meals and recumbency, relieved by antacids.
- Musculoskeletal chest wall pain (costochondritis) is characterized by sharp localized tenderness reproducible upon direct palpation of the costochondral junctions, worsened by chest wall movement or deep inspiration.
- Pericarditis is suggested by sharp, pleuritic chest pain that improves when leaning forward and worsens in the supine position, often with a pericardial friction rub on auscultation and diffuse PR-segment depression on ECG.
